- <a id="st-b28e05feed83" class="st-a"></a>**Switzerland's autumn parliamentary session opened on Monday 14 September, and the Council of States (the smaller chamber, which represents the cantons) is set to hold the first-ever floor debate on the Bilaterals III package — the roughly 2,000-page treaty deal renewing Switzerland's relationship with the EU.** The chamber has reserved three session days for it and must decide, among other things, whether to place the package under a *mandatory* referendum, which would require a double majority of both voters and cantons to pass before it could take effect (an optional referendum would need only a majority of voters). The three-week session, which runs to 2 October, also queues up several other contested files: a proposed "armament fund" bankrolled by a temporary VAT rise — the Federal Council wants 0.5 percentage points for 12 years, while the Council of States' security committee counters with 0.2 points spread over 18 years — a watered-down "Lex UBS" over how much hard capital the bank must hold against its foreign subsidiaries after the Credit Suisse collapse, a 517-million-franc farm-support compromise meant to salvage the Mercosur free-trade agreement the National Council rejected in the summer session, and a revision of the Epidemics Act that would give the federal government standing authority to order nationwide measures such as a transport mask mandate. Why it matters: the Bilaterals III vote is the defining question for how Switzerland trades with and moves within its largest partner for a generation, and the referendum-type decision taken now shapes how high the bar will be at the ballot box.
