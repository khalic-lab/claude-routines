- <a id="st-8cacb70b21a8" class="st-a"></a>**Estonia will close its road border crossings with Russia on Tuesday 1 December, its foreign ministry said on Sunday 15 November. The move follows an attempted arson attack on a drone maker near Tallinn that Estonia's Internal Security Service (KAPO) attributes to Russia's military intelligence service, the GRU.** Eight Latvian citizens were detained after trying to break into the premises of Threod Systems in Viimsi on Saturday morning, 14 November, with the aim of causing an explosion and a fire, according to the public broadcaster ERR. Threod makes long-range reconnaissance and attack drones. This is the second attempted arson attack on an Estonian defence firm since August, when Milrem Robotics was targeted. The three crossings affected, Narva, Luhamaa and Koidula, are among the last land routes into Russia still open to travellers from the EU. Russian foreign ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova said that, if confirmed, the plan would be "unbridled Russophobia". The attribution to the GRU comes from Estonian authorities and has not been independently verified.   - Why it matters: an EU and NATO state is shutting a land border in response to sabotage against its arms industry. It is the clearest step yet by a Baltic state from naming Russia's hybrid attacks to imposing a concrete cost for them.

Lawyers for former detainees at the US Center for Constitutional Rights had argued that the evidence of a torture policy, including the US Senate's own report, was overwhelming. Le Monde says Washington had made closing the case one of its conditions for not sanctioning the Court ahead of April 2027. [single-source] ([Le Monde, 16 Nov 2026](https://www.lemonde.fr/international/article/2026/11/16/cpi-des-juges-referment-le-dossier-sur-les-crimes-des-forces-americaines-en-afghanistan_6811690_3210.html))
  - Why it matters: this was one of the few avenues for international accountability for US abuses after 9/11. It is closing at a moment when the US is pressuring the Court. The judges, for their part, gave procedural reasons: the time that has passed and the limits on gathering evidence.

- <a id="st-e09cb310fc52" class="st-a"></a>**Switzerland registered 2,210 asylum applications in October 2026, 16% fewer than in October 2025 (433 fewer), the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) said on Monday 16 November.** Afghanistan remained the main country of origin with 498 applications, followed by Eritrea (430), Algeria (236), Ethiopia (171) and Somalia (163). Applications rose by 88 (4%) from September, which the SEM says is the usual late-summer and autumn rise before winter. The SEM decided 2,419 cases at first instance and granted asylum to 567 people. A total of 732 people left Switzerland under official supervision: 418 on their own and 314 under police escort, either to their home country, to the EU state responsible under the Dublin rules, or to another country.
