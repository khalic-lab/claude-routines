- <a id="st-7d159e7f9ee2" class="st-a"></a>**Switzerland is releasing diesel from its compulsory emergency stocks after pipeline deliveries were delayed by heavy rain in southern France, the Federal Council said on Friday 23 October.** Roland Pfister, the federal delegate for national economic supply, authorised withdrawals of up to 45,000 m³ between 23 October and Sunday 22 November. That is just under 4% of the current compulsory diesel stock. The government says supply "remains assured". It points to oil markets that have been tight since the start of the war in Iran, and to months of low water on the Rhine, which has disrupted shipping to Switzerland, so that any hitch in another transport route quickly becomes a bottleneck. This follows a separate release of kerosene, which the communiqué says is authorised until Sunday 1 November. Petrol and heating oil are covered for now, but the situation is "tense and precarious for all petroleum products". France is doing the same: a decree published on Saturday 24 October allows the sale of up to 1.6 million m³ of strategic diesel stocks until 31 January to bring pump prices down. Why it matters: drivers, hauliers and farmers should see no shortage, but Switzerland now has two emergency fuel releases running at once, which shows how little slack is left in its supply chain going into winter.
