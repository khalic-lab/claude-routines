- <a id="st-82f1b7219d83" class="st-a"></a>**Switzerland has stopped importing beef and poultry from Brazil since 3 September, aligning with an EU ban imposed because Brazil could not give sufficient guarantees on antibiotic use in livestock, the Federal Food Safety and Veterinary Office (BLV) confirmed.** EU rules bar antibiotics for growth promotion and ban so-called reserve antibiotics — those kept in reserve for human medicine — in farm animals entirely, to slow the spread of antibiotic resistance. The effect on Swiss shelves is uneven: nearly 80% of the beef eaten in Switzerland is home-produced and most imports come from Germany and Austria, so the beef impact is minimal, whereas about 40% of poultry is imported and roughly a third of that came from Brazil. The industry body Proviande calls the poultry situation manageable but does not rule out price rises. **Why it matters:** It is a concrete, if modest, cost-of-living and food-safety story — a rule change made in Brussels and mirrored in Bern that could nudge up chicken prices and shows how closely Swiss import standards track the EU's. ([SRF, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.srf.ch/sendungen/kassensturz-espresso/espresso/rindfleisch-und-gefluegel-schweiz-stoppt-fleisch-aus-brasilien-wegen-antibiotika))
