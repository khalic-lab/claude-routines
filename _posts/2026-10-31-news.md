- <a id="st-7ed6a1bd7ae4" class="st-a"></a>**The UN Security Council on Friday 30 October failed to renew the UN peacekeeping mission in Western Sahara (MINURSO), so its 35-year mandate ends on Saturday 31 October.** Russia vetoed a US draft that got 11 votes in favour, with 3 abstentions including China. The US draft would have extended the mission for a year, dropped "Referendum" from its name and made Morocco's 2007 autonomy plan the basis for talks. A Russian counter-draft for a plain one-year renewal then got only 3 votes, with the US voting against. MINURSO was set up in 1991 to monitor a ceasefire and organise a vote on independence or integration with Morocco, but the vote never took place. Morocco's royal palace called the outcome a "significant step" towards closing the file and said UN staff should leave. The Polisario Front (the Algeria-backed independence movement) welcomed the veto. US ambassador Mike Waltz said he was ready to work on an "orderly withdrawal", while France's delegate warned that an abrupt closure would be "exceedingly alarming" given renewed tensions. Why it matters: the Council rarely lets a peacekeeping mission lapse this way.
