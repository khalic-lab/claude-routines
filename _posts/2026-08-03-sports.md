### FIFA drops its World Cup sell-off — and Infantino is suddenly fighting for his job {#st-53cf8a3011d3}
**[A bruising 24 hours: what next for Fifa and under-pressure Infantino? — BBC Sport, 2 August 2026](https://www.bbc.com/sport/football/articles/c9w0djx2g92o)** · FIFA governance · 28 July–2 August

FIFA scrapped its most contentious commercial project in years, and its Swiss president, Gianni Infantino, ended the week fighting to keep his job. On 31 July, football's Zurich-based world governing body abandoned a plan — unveiled only days earlier, on 28 July — to move its tournaments into a new commercial company, FIFA Forward Enterprises (FFE), and sell a minority stake (reported at up to 21%) to private investors to raise around $4 billion for its 211 member associations. The revolt was near-total: UEFA said all 55 of its European members would oppose it, CONCACAF's 41 members formally rejected it too, and by FIFA's own admission the scheme had "fractured" the game and was no longer viable — so it was killed before any vote. The backlash then turned personal: days later the Football Association of Wales became the first member association to publicly withdraw its support for Infantino's re-election, citing failures of "governance, leadership and judgement," with other associations weighing similar moves.
*Why it matters:* Selling equity in the World Cup would have handed private financiers a lasting claim on football's most valuable asset. The opposition — spanning well over half of FIFA's 211 members — and Infantino's rare, humiliating U-turn are the clearest limit yet on how far the sport's Zurich power centre can commercialise the game over its members' objections.

Australia comfortably topped the medal table as the Commonwealth Games closed in Glasgow on 2 August, finishing with roughly 70 golds and about 170 medals in all — well clear of England, second on around 110 medals, and Canada in third. The bigger story was the event itself: this was a deliberately pared-back Games, cut to about ten sports across a handful of venues after the Australian state of Victoria abandoned its hosting deal in 2023 and Glasgow stepped in to stop the Commonwealth Games collapsing entirely. Organisers called the compact model a success and a template for a cheaper future; sceptics counter that a single-city, ten-sport edition still leaves unanswered whether a 90-plus-nation event rooted in the British Empire has a lasting place on the modern sporting calendar. *Why it matters:* This was less a contest than a survival exercise for one of world sport's oldest multi-sport events — and it worked, but only by shrinking. The question it leaves is whether "smaller and cheaper" is a genuine future or just a stay of execution.
