- <a id="st-71f3aaa187db" class="st-a"></a>**The 2026 heat-and-drought summer is inflicting damage on Swiss forests that long-term monitors say they have never recorded, even as rivers and springs run dry.** New field surveys by the Institut für angewandte Pflanzenbiologie (IAP — an applied-plant-biology institute that has monitored Swiss forest health for various cantons since 1974) found that in the beech stands it tracks, two-thirds of the trees are now severely damaged — browning, shedding leaves, or with badly thinned crowns — nearly three times the count after the previous record-heat summer of 2018. The institute's Sabine Braun, who inspects roughly 12,000 trees across 23 cantons each year, said leaves are now drying on the branch and even green ones are "getting sunburnt" — a phenomenon she says she has not seen in 42 years of measurements. Because only about a quarter of the beeches badly hit in 2018 survived, and it is the accumulation of dry years that kills, researchers warn the beech — Switzerland's commonest broadleaf — could all but vanish from the northwest. The drought is visible elsewhere too: Basel's Rhine passenger ferry "Wild Maa" has been beached because the river is too weak to run it, and in peripheral regions springs have dried up, forcing water to be trucked in by tank. Why it matters: forest composition, drinking-water supply and even summer traditions are shifting inside a single season, and the survey turns an abstract "hot summer" into a measured, hard-to-reverse ecological change on the ground.
