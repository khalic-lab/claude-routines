- <a id="st-be3fdf9fb052" class="st-a"></a>**On Friday 4 September the Council of States' preparatory committees advanced the "Bilaterals III" treaty package with the EU — but reaffirmed that it should have to clear a double hurdle to pass.** The relevant committee recommended the government's package — 18 treaties meant to stabilise and extend Switzerland's relations with the EU — for adoption by 9 votes to 3 with one abstention. Separately, the chamber's state-policy committee (SPK-S — the Council of States body that handles domestic implementation) reaffirmed, on the casting vote of its president Heidi Z'graggen (Mitte), that the package must require a **double majority (Volks- und Ständemehr — approval by both a majority of voters and a majority of the 26 cantons)**, and would write a transitional clause into the constitution to that end; the National Council's counterpart committee has backed the same demand. A cantonal majority is a markedly higher bar than the simple popular vote the Federal Council originally envisaged, because it over-weights small rural cantons; opponents led by the SVP have pushed for it precisely because it makes the deal easier to defeat at the ballot box. The full Council of States takes up the package in the autumn session, with a first vote expected by the end of September. Why it matters: whether the EU package needs one majority or two could decide the fate of a deal that would shape free movement, market access and Swiss–EU relations for a generation. - <a id="st-86d25a920dde" class="st-a"></a>**A Graubünden regional court acquitted all five defendants on Friday 4 September in the trial over the 2017 Bondo rockslide, which killed eight hikers.** [ongoing since 2026-08-24] The Regionalgericht Maloja, sitting in Pontresina, cleared the former mayor of Bregaglia and FDP national councillor Anna Giacometti, a municipal forester, two cantonal officials (a geologist and a forestry engineer) and an external geologist, all charged in connection with negligent homicide. The court found no proof of a specific breach of duty of care: in the weeks before the 23 August 2017 collapse at Piz Cengalo there were no clear signs of a foreseeable major event, and — invoking "in dubio pro reo" (when in doubt, for the accused) — it held the calculations and warnings in place at the time were defensible. Lead prosecutor Bruno Ulmi Stuppani said an appeal was still open and would be discussed internally. Why it matters: the verdict draws a hard line on when officials can be held criminally liable for failing to foresee a natural-hazard disaster, and reopens the question of who is responsible for danger assessment between cantons and municipalities.
