- <a id="st-85384c4b7250" class="st-a"></a>**The Federal Supreme Court ruled, in a judgment published on Thursday 15 October, that the Federal Department of Finance was allowed to cut or cancel the outstanding bonuses of former Credit Suisse managers, overturning the Federal Administrative Court.** In May 2023, after the state-backed takeover of Credit Suisse by UBS, the department ordered the bonuses cancelled for the executive board and cut by 50% and 25% for the two levels below. About 1,000 people were affected. Several managers challenged the order, and in 2025 the Federal Administrative Court agreed with them, saying contractually promised bonuses needed a clearer legal basis. The Supreme Court disagreed. It said Article 10a of the Banking Act explicitly lets the government act on pay when a systemically important bank gets state support, and that this covers cancelling unpaid bonuses. The government had given Credit Suisse a default guarantee of up to 100 billion francs. The court said public money must not fund bonuses for the period when the crisis built up, and that the later repayment of liquidity aid does not change that. It also said the cuts are preventive, not a punishment, so no manager's personal responsibility had to be proven. Why it matters: it confirms the state's power to block bonuses at any bank it rescues, while the Federal Council's proposed tighter rules on bank pay and manager accountability are still in consultation. — [SRF, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/nach-cs-untergang-bundesgericht-doch-keine-boni-fuer-cs-kader); [watson (ATS), 15 Oct 2026](https://www.watson.ch/fr/suisse/economie/901079520-les-anciens-cadres-de-credit-suisse-peuvent-oublier-leurs-bonus) [new source]
