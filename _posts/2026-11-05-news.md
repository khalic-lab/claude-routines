- <a id="st-926ce1ced31b" class="st-a"></a>**The Federal Council on Wednesday 4 November proposed a reform of disability insurance (AI/IV) that would raise payroll contributions by 0.2 percentage points, split between employees and employers, and opened a consultation that runs until Thursday 4 February 2027.** The government says the insurance has run a structural deficit since the early 1990s: its pay-as-you-go deficit was over CHF 200 million in 2025 and could reach CHF 600 million in 2030, its fund now covers only 33% of a year's spending against a legal minimum of 50%, and without action it would be empty by 2031. The main new measure is an "integration benefit" for 18- to 24-year-olds, mostly with mental illnesses, who are not yet fit for vocational rehabilitation, an estimated 1,000 people a year or about a third of new pensions in that age group. Savings of CHF 150 million a year by 2030 would come partly from raising the disability degree needed for a full pension from 70% to 80% and cutting the daily allowance for people without dependants. On top of the permanent 0.2-point rise (about CHF 980 million in 2030), from 2030 to 2039 earnings above CHF 181,440 would pay an extra 0.6 points, and part of the casino tax would move from AHV/AVS to the disability insurance. Why it matters: almost every payslip in Switzerland would carry a slightly higher deduction, and people with severe but not total disability would need a higher assessed degree to get a full pension.
