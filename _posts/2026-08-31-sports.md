### The FIA reopens the door to Russia, and Ukraine objects {#st-c449b8bc8a21}
**[Ukraine files complaint to FIA ethics committee after Russian ban lift — Autosport](https://www.autosport.com/general/news/ukraine-files-complaint-to-fia-ethics-committee-after-russian-ban-lift/10850119/)** · 27 August 2026
Motorsport's governing body, the FIA, has **lifted the ban on Russian and Belarusian competitors** in international events — a ban in place since Russia's February 2022 invasion of Ukraine. The move, approved by the FIA's World Council and reportedly proposed by president Mohammed Ben Sulayem, drew an immediate **formal complaint to the FIA ethics committee from the Ukrainian Automobile Federation (FAU)**, which is demanding an urgent investigation of its Russian counterpart. The FAU says the Russian federation operates offices in Russian-occupied parts of Ukraine — Crimea, Sevastopol, and the Donetsk and Zaporizhzhia regions, all UN-recognised as Ukrainian territory — in breach of FIA statutes that permit only one motoring club per country.
*Why it matters:* Sport keeps being asked to referee geopolitics. The FIA's decision would let Russian drivers back onto international grids while the war continues, and Ukraine is framing it as a governance breach rather than a sporting one.

### The Vuelta is blown open: race leader Pogačar crashes out {#st-5debe8b256d2}
**[Mas proves more than up to La Roja — official stage 9 report, lavuelta.es](https://www.lavuelta.es/en/news/2026/stage-9/mas-proves-more-than-up-to-la-roja/59672)** `[new source]` · Vuelta a España, stages 8–9 · 29–30 August 2026
A week after the Vuelta a España — the third and final of cycling's three-week Grand Tours — opened with **Tadej Pogačar** in command, the world champion is out. Pogačar, the overwhelming favourite chasing the one Grand Tour missing from his career, **crashed with about 33km left of Saturday's eighth stage and abandoned the race**; his UAE Team Emirates doctor reported a broken left collarbone requiring surgery (delayed for concussion precautions), a concussion and a "stable" neck fracture. It is the first Grand Tour he has ever failed to finish. He had led by 3 minutes 50 seconds. Spain's **Enric Mas** inherited the leader's red jersey — declining to wear it on Saturday's podium out of respect — and then won Sunday's ninth stage atop the Aitana climb, extending his overall lead (the general classification, i.e. cumulative time) to 1:18 over Primož Roglič, with Félix Gall third and Britain's Oscar Onley up to fourth.
*Why it matters:* The most dominant stage racer of his generation was removed from a race he was strangling — and a Grand Tour that looked finished is suddenly a genuine contest with two weeks still to ride.

### Werro closes on the oldest record in athletics {#st-06046d78f377}
**[Weltklasse Zürich — meet news and results](https://weltklassezuerich.ch/en/news/)** `[new source]` · Zurich Diamond League, Letzigrund Stadium · 27 August 2026
On a record-breaking night at the Zurich Diamond League (the elite one-day meeting series), Swiss European champion **Audrey Werro ran 1:53.70 over 800m — the third-fastest time in history** — finishing just 0.42 seconds shy of Jarmila Kratochvilová's 1:53.28, set in 1983 and the **oldest outdoor world record in athletics**. Roared on by a 25,000-strong home crowd, the 22-year-old set a personal best and now holds four of the ten fastest 800m times ever run, all recorded this summer. She was not alone in threatening the history books: the same meeting produced **two world records** — Alison dos Santos in the 400m hurdles and Russell in the 100m hurdles.
*Why it matters:* A 43-year-old record that has outlasted whole eras of the sport now looks beatable, and a Swiss athlete on home soil is the one making it look inevitable — "sooner rather than later," in her own read of the race.
