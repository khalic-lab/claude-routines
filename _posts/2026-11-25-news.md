- <a id="st-1a6778b3f75a" class="st-a"></a>**The Grand Chamber of the European Court of Human Rights ruled on Tuesday 24 November that when a hostile state deliberately sends migrants across a border, returning them to that state is not in itself a violation of the ban on inhuman treatment, but border authorities must first identify people who are especially at risk.** The three cases involved Afghans, Iraqi Kurds and Cubans who tried to enter Poland, Latvia and Lithuania from Belarus in 2021–22. The court used the term "instrumentalisation of migration" for this tactic of pushing migrants towards a neighbour's border to destabilise it. It said states may restrict entry or even close their borders entirely, and that they need not check whether migrants would have access to asylum in Belarus. Before returning anyone, however, they must screen for two groups: people with evident vulnerabilities (age, illness, disability) and people with a specific basis to claim a real and immediate risk of ill-treatment. The court unanimously found violations for one Afghan applicant whom Poland removed to Belarus on 20 October 2021 despite an interim court order, and for the children and their accompanying families in the Latvian case. It found no violation for the Latvian adults and, by 11 votes to 6, none in the Lithuanian case. ([ECHR press release, 24 Nov 2026](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/eng-press?i=003-8704399-12426091) [official PR]; [DW, 24 Nov 2026](https://www.dw.com/en/european-court-finds-states-can-turn-back-migrants-in-some-cases/a-80038352))
  - Why it matters: this is the first time Europe's top human-rights court has set out the rules for "weaponised" migration, and the standard applies in all 46 Council of Europe states, Switzerland among them.
