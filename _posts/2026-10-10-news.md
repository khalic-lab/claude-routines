- <a id="st-fab92630547f" class="st-a"></a>**The United States put the International Criminal Court itself on its sanctions list on Friday 9 October, hours after a former ICC judge, Navi Pillay, won the Nobel Peace Prize.** The Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) added the court in The Hague to its list of blocked entities. It also issued four general licences, carve-outs that still allow some dealings with the court, including telecoms and enterprise software, pension payments and matters concerning ICC detainees. Washington had already sanctioned 13 of the court's judges and prosecutors, the ICC said. Le Monde reports that the measures only take full effect on Friday 9 April 2027, unless the court meets US demands first: cancel its arrest warrants for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and former defence minister Yoav Gallant, close its investigation into alleged US and CIA abuses linked to Afghanistan, and amend its statute to bar prosecuting nationals of non-member states. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said that if allies did not "rein in the court" the US would dismantle it "brick by brick". The ICC called the sanctions "an assault on the rule of law". Canada, Denmark, Germany, France, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands and the UK said jointly that they "strongly disagree". Netanyahu thanked Washington. The US is not a party to the court's founding Rome Statute, which 125 states have ratified. Why it matters: blocking the court as an institution reaches its bank accounts, IT suppliers and staff pay. That turns the US demands into a deadline for the European governments that host and fund the court.
