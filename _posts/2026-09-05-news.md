- <a id="st-ac111c68ae78" class="st-a"></a>**The UN General Assembly on Friday 4 September voted 164 to 1 to encourage governments, schools and technology companies to drop maps that make Africa look far smaller than it is.** The resolution — the result of an African-led campaign fronted by Togo — promotes the Equal Earth projection and other equal-area maps over the 16th-century Mercator projection, which preserves shapes and navigation angles but inflates landmasses near the poles (making Greenland look roughly the size of Africa, which is about 14 times larger). It does not ban Mercator or mandate a replacement, only urges its use "when relative size matters." The United States cast the lone opposing vote, dismissing the measure as part of a "radical ideological project"; Estonia, Georgia, Lithuania, Moldova, Serbia and Ukraine abstained. Why it matters: how the world is drawn shapes what schoolchildren absorb about it, and the near-unanimous vote — with the US isolated — is itself a small map of current diplomatic alignments.
