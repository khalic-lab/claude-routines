- <a id="st-c297aaeeac22" class="st-a"></a>**The Council of States voted on Wednesday 23 September to require UBS to back its foreign subsidiaries with 90% Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1 — the highest-quality, loss-absorbing "hard" capital), rejecting a softer compromise from its own committee.** The chamber (Ständerat — the cantonal chamber of parliament) discarded a 50/50 formula floated by its Economic Affairs Committee, under which UBS could have met half the requirement with cheaper AT1 bonds (Additional Tier 1 — debt that converts to equity or is written down in a crisis). Finance Minister Karin Keller-Sutter had argued the 50/50 version was legally shaky because AT1 instruments are not aligned with international regulatory standards, and financial-market regulator FINMA and the Swiss National Bank both back the Federal Council's tougher line as sensible and bearable for the bank. UBS itself puts the recurring cost of the stricter capital rules at close to 3 billion francs a year, and CEO Sergio Ermotti has argued they would undercut the bank's international competitiveness; the size of that price is disputed among economists. The 90% decision is a first reading and still has to clear the National Council and reconciliation. Why it matters: this is the core fight of Switzerland's post–Credit Suisse "too-big-to-fail" overhaul — how much of a buffer the country's only remaining global bank must hold so taxpayers aren't again on the hook for a rescue, weighed against the cost to a bank whose balance sheet dwarfs the Swiss economy. The committee found that at every point the available information showed there was no agreement on a lump-sum fixed price, and dismissed as baseless the argument that Washington had contractually agreed one. Responsibilities for the misleading communication remain to be assigned. Why it matters: the F-35 buy is Switzerland's largest-ever arms procurement and was sold to voters on a cost promise the state's own oversight body now says was never real — a credibility problem for the defence department heading into further budget fights.
