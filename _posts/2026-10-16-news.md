- <a id="st-ec6f1f9d959a" class="st-a"></a>**The federal delegate for economic supply, Roland Pfister, on Thursday 15 October let fuel companies draw up to 30,000 m³ of kerosene (jet fuel) from Switzerland's compulsory reserves between 15 October and 1 November, because Zurich airport risks running short.** The amount is 6.5% of the kerosene now held in those reserves. The cause is rail freight: many deliveries on the lines from Germany have been cancelled or delayed, and the low Rhine has pushed other goods from barges onto the already busy rail network. The Federal Office for National Economic Supply (FONES) says the airport's own stocks would not cover the missing deliveries. It adds that supply of petrol, diesel and heating oil has worsened since last week but is still secure. Why it matters: Switzerland's emergency fuel stocks are now being drawn on for a logistics bottleneck, low water on the Rhine and saturated rail, not only for a supply shock abroad.
