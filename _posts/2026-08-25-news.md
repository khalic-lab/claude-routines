- <a id="st-3e39b5dc1af9" class="st-a"></a>**The Valais village of Randa sealed off a danger zone on Tuesday 25 August after warning that a hanging glacier above the village could break away within days.** The commune said monitoring of the Weisshorn Ost hanging glacier (a mass of ice clinging to a steep slope, prone to sudden collapse) had detected the ice moving faster, and it has stepped up observation and defined a no-go hazard zone that must not be entered until further notice. According to the commune's map, only a few individual buildings fall inside the zone; most of the village lies just outside but very close to it. Randa knows the risk: in spring 1991 one of the largest rockfalls in 20th-century Europe — some 30 million cubic metres — crashed toward the valley here, damming the Mattervispa river and flooding parts of the village, and the disaster spurred Switzerland to develop early-warning technology for rockfalls. The warning also comes months after the glacier collapse that buried the village of Blatten. Why it matters: it is a direct, immediate hazard for residents of the Mattertal (the valley linking Visp to Zermatt), and another instance of the accelerating Alpine instability that Swiss mountain communities are now managing in real time.
