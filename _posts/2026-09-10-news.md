- <a id="st-80b1b8331f25" class="st-a"></a>**The European Parliament's foreign affairs committee (AFET) approved the Switzerland–EU "Bilaterals III" package on Thursday 10 September by 53 votes to 3**, a lopsided endorsement on the EU side of a treaty that has yet to clear either parliament's plenary. Christophe Grudler, the French Renew MEP who is the file's rapporteur, called the vote an "étape importante" (important step) and said Brussels has no "plan B" if the deal fails. It follows the Swiss Council of States' committees, which on 4 September advanced the package on the domestic side but insisted it must pass a double majority of voters and cantons — so the ratification road still runs through a Swiss popular vote. Why it matters: Bilaterals III governs free movement, the single market and research access that directly shape how people in Switzerland live, work and study with the EU; a strong committee signal in Brussels narrows the question to whether Swiss voters will ratify. - <a id="st-fa5a401a3aa7" class="st-a"></a>**The Federal Council has come out in favour of keeping the so-called "Lex On," recommending that parliament reject two motions that sought to undo a March amendment to the Swissness law.** The change lets companies add the Swiss cross to labels such as "Swiss engineering" or "Swiss design" under certain conditions even when the product itself is made abroad — the Zurich brand On, whose shoes are manufactured in Asia, is the emblematic case. The government says it will accompany the looser practice with an "evaluation of the Swissness provisions." The two reversal motions came from PLR politician Daniela Schneeberger and Centre politician Fabio Regazzi; industrial circles, including in Vaud, have called the loosening a "dilution" of a "fabulous" asset and, in the sharpest framing, a "trahison" (betrayal) of firms that actually produce in Switzerland. Why it matters: the Swiss cross is a paid-for marketing premium, and who may use it — genuine domestic manufacturers or brands that design here but build elsewhere — is a direct commercial fight over the value of "made in Switzerland." [[Le Temps, 10 Sept 2026](https://www.letemps.ch/economie/lex-on-le-conseil-federal-desavoue-les-pme-et-les-milieux-industriels)]

- <a id="st-8d029c797abc" class="st-a"></a>**Switzerland's housing vacancy rate fell to 0.93% as of 1 June — 45,493 empty dwellings — the Federal Statistical Office (OFS/BFS) reported on Thursday**, down 2,962 units (−6.1%) in a year and the continuation of a squeeze under way since 2021. Why it matters: a sub-1% vacancy rate means tighter searches and upward pressure on rents across most of the country — a direct cost-of-living squeeze for anyone moving or renewing a lease.
