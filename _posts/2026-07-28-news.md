AT1 bonds ("additional tier 1" — subordinated debt created after the 2008 crisis that is designed to absorb losses and prop up a failing bank) worth about US$17.3 billion were written off when the Swiss state orchestrated UBS's emergency takeover of Credit Suisse in spring 2023. A group of international investors had sued in June 2024 for roughly US$370 million; they can still seek review by the full appeals court or the US Supreme Court, though the EFD says the bar for that is "very high." Separate cases continue elsewhere: Switzerland's own Federal Administrative Court partly overturned the write-down in October 2025 after complaints from some 3,600 investors — a ruling FINMA and UBS have appealed to the Federal Supreme Court — and a group of Japanese investors filed an arbitration claim at the ICSID (a World Bank body that settles investment disputes) in late 2025. **Why it matters:** the US ruling closes one front in the global legal fight over the most contested piece of the CS rescue, but the decisive battles over whether the write-down stands are still open in Swiss courts and international arbitration.
