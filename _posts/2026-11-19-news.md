- <a id="st-a011a10555c0" class="st-a"></a>**The International Court of Justice (ICJ) ruled on Thursday 19 November that Myanmar acted with genocidal intent against the Rohingya in its 2017 military campaign, deciding the case The Gambia brought in 2019 under the 1948 Genocide Convention.** Reading the judgment over about three hours, Court President Yuji Iwasawa said the evidence showed that Myanmar's security forces killed some 10,000 Rohingya, committed widespread sexual violence and extreme violence against children, and attacked people as they fled to Bangladesh. The court said the "only reasonable inference" from the pattern of atrocities was an intent to destroy a "substantial part" of the group. It also found that Myanmar "violated its obligation to prevent genocide" by letting armed civilians join the campaign. Myanmar had argued that the operation responded to attacks on police posts by the Arakan Rohingya Salvation Army. The crackdown drove more than 730,000 Rohingya into Bangladesh, and about 1.2 million now live in camps around Cox's Bazar. ICJ judgments are final and cannot be appealed, but the court has no means of enforcing them itself. [ICJ case page, The Gambia v.   - Why it matters: Swiss broadcaster SRF calls it the first time the UN's highest court has found a state responsible for genocide. In 2007, in Bosnia's case against Serbia, the court found only that Serbia had failed to prevent genocide at Srebrenica. The ruling gives the Rohingya a legal finding they can use in claims for return, reparations and prosecutions, and it is a precedent for other genocide cases now before the court.
