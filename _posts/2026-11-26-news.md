- <a id="st-4db002f66a0a" class="st-a"></a>**The US Trade Representative (USTR) proposed on Wednesday 25 November an extra tariff of up to 15% on goods from Switzerland and four other economies, after finding that 14 trading partners' "structural excess capacity" in manufacturing is actionable under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974.** Section 301 lets the USTR impose duties on practices it deems "unreasonable" and harmful to US commerce. The 14 economies include Switzerland, the EU, China, Japan, Korea and Taiwan, with proposed rates from up to 15% (Switzerland, the EU, Japan, Korea, Taiwan) to up to 35% (Vietnam). Most notices also name a higher alternative rate if USTR judges consultations unsatisfactory, according to a summary by the law firm Holland & Knight. Nothing takes effect yet: written comments are due Tuesday 5 January 2027 and hearings begin Monday 25 January 2027. SRF reports that for Swiss exports this would mean 15% next year instead of the 12.5% now charged under the July 2026 forced-labour tariffs. That is the same level as the EU and within the November 2025 Swiss–US declaration of intent, far below the 39% Switzerland faced in summer 2025. The State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) rejects the US allegations, SRF reports, and Swissmem called them "absurd". These two Section 301 actions largely replace the tariffs the US Supreme Court struck down in February 2026. SRF's economics editor called the new duties "factually unfounded". ([Holland & Knight, 25 Nov 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/11/ustr-proposes-tariffs-following-14-section-301); [SRF, 25 Nov 2026](https://www.srf.ch/news/international/erhoehung-bis-zu-15-prozent-was-die-neu-angekuendigten-us-zoelle-fuer-die-schweiz-bedeuten))
  - Why it matters: Swiss exporters face a further 2.5-point rise from 2027 unless Bern's negotiations produce the binding 15% ceiling it is seeking. The rate would match the EU's, so Swiss firms would at least compete with their main rivals on equal terms.

- <a id="st-9c7f679560f7" class="st-a"></a>**The European Commission fined the Swiss fragrance makers Givaudan and Firmenich and the US group IFF a combined €472 million on Wednesday 25 November for a cartel, and the UK Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) added £85 million the same day.** The Commission says the three, which supply about half of Europe's €4 billion-plus market for fragrances in detergents, shampoos and cleaning products, exchanged commercially sensitive information, including on prices, rather than competing. According to Le Temps/AFP, the conduct ran from March 2019 until the Commission's dawn raids in March 2023. In the EU, Givaudan was fined about €252.4 million, Firmenich €137.7 million and IFF €81.9 million. The CMA says the companies shared information to refuse requests from a major customer, Unilever, including for price cuts, agreed not to poach each other's staff on that account, and in some cases met at in-person "beer and curry" meetings in London. All three admitted the conduct and settled. Competition chief Teresa Ribera said the Swiss Competition Commission (COMCO) and the US Justice Department also worked on the case. ([European Commission, 25 Nov 2026](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/speech_26_2589) [official PR]; [CMA via gov.uk, 25 Nov 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/major-fragrance-suppliers-fined-85-million-by-the-cma-for-illegal-collusion) [official PR]; [Le Temps/AFP, 26 Nov 2026](https://www.letemps.ch/economie/entreprises/firmenich-givaudan-et-iff-sont-sanctionnes-par-l-europe-pour-avoir-coordonne-leurs-prix))
  - Why it matters: fragrances can make up to 10% of the cost of a bottle of detergent or shampoo, the Commission says, so the overcharge may have reached shoppers. COMCO's involvement leaves open whether a Swiss decision follows.

- <a id="st-2fb8a93f4e33" class="st-a"></a>**The Federal Council sent parliament its counter-proposal to the popular initiative "for responsible large companies" on Wednesday 25 November: a corporate sustainability law that it has narrowed since consultation.** The Federal Council says specific human-rights and environmental due-diligence duties would apply to between 30 and 60 large Swiss companies, which must assess their risks and act on them. It promises no stricter rules than the EU's, except on child labour, where Swiss rules are already stricter. Fewer firms would have to report than today: the sustainability-reporting duty would cover about 110 large companies, almost half as many as now. Compared with the consultation draft, the range of sanctions has been narrowed and climate reports no longer go to shareholders' meetings but to an external auditor. Liability rests on ordinary fault-based liability (art. 41 of the Code of Obligations): the injured party carries the burden of proof, and companies are not liable for damage caused solely by their business partners. ([Federal Council via admin.ch, 25 Nov 2026](https://www.admin.ch/fr/newnsb/Prvb4ekrdHsH) [official PR])
  - Why it matters: parliament now weighs a law that adds due-diligence duties for a few dozen firms while shrinking reporting for the rest, against an initiative that wants more. That is the choice voters will eventually face.

- <a id="st-88de88af704c" class="st-a"></a>**The Federal Council sent parliament a bill on Wednesday 25 November to make accident insurance cover victims of sexual violence in every case, including when they were drugged.** Under current law, Swiss social insurance counts an injury as an accident only if it is "sudden" and involuntary. Some victims of drug-facilitated assault cannot prove suddenness, so they currently get no accident-insurance benefits. The amendment to the Accident Insurance Act (LAA) would always treat health damage from sexual assault or coercion as an accident, even if the victim could not resist or was not aware, covering medical costs and daily allowances for lost earnings. The draft was very well received in consultation between 1 April and 7 July 2026. ([Federal Council via admin.ch, 25 Nov 2026](https://www.admin.ch/fr/newnsb/f6CupdOvTdWq) [official PR])

- <a id="st-fe6d78ff62ae" class="st-a"></a>**Swiss employment grew 1.2% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, to 5.684 million jobs, but fell 0.3% from the previous quarter and companies' hiring plans weakened, the Federal Statistical Office (FSO) said on Thursday 26 November.** Seasonally adjusted, companies shed 19,800 jobs in the quarter, 18,200 of them in services. The share of employment in firms planning to cut staff rose to 5.1% from 3.9%, while the share in firms planning to hire fell to 10.6% from 11.3%. Vacancies still rose 3.3% on the year to 93,900, and 34.2% of firms reported difficulty hiring skilled staff. The release follows the 12 November figures showing unemployment by the International Labour Organization's definition at 5.6%. ([FSO via admin.ch, 26 Nov 2026](https://www.admin.ch/fr/newnsb/X83dUsjjR4Lp) [official PR])
