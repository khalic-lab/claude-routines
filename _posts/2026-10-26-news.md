In his victory speech Lula, who turns 81 on Tuesday 27 October, said voters had defeated the "interference" of digital platforms and promised to regulate them. He will face the most conservative Congress since Brazil's return to democracy in the 1980s: Bolsonaro's Liberal Party and its allies could hold more than 280 of 513 lower-house seats. Why it matters: Latin America's largest country stays with a left-wing government that has clashed with Washington over trade, but a hostile Congress and a contested result may limit what Lula can do in his fourth term. - <a id="st-b70704949369" class="st-a"></a>**Migros announced on Monday 26 October that it plans to merge its ten regional cooperatives into a single group cooperative, the biggest change to its structure since founder Gottlieb Duttweiler chose a decentralised model in the 1940s.** The boards of all ten cooperatives and of the Federation of Migros Cooperatives approved the plan unanimously. The 2.3 million cooperative members will vote on it in mid-2027, and it needs a two-thirds majority in each of the ten regions. The supermarket business would be run nationally, and the regional leadership structures would be replaced by six regional councils, including one for French-speaking Switzerland and one for Ticino. Migros gave no figures for savings or job losses. The SonntagsZeitung reported that about 3,000 jobs could go and that the savings could reach CHF 300–400 million a year. Migros says the changes mainly affect administration and management, not shop staff, and promises no restructuring-related layoffs in the regional cooperatives until at least mid-2028. Group chief Mario Irminger and supermarket head Peter Diethelm will step down at the time of the members' vote. Why it matters: Switzerland's largest retailer, with about 100,000 staff, is reorganising after selling Hotelplan, Melectronics and other businesses since 2024, and the job cuts would fall in regional head offices across the country, including Vaud.
