- <a id="st-681e25b63c90" class="st-a"></a>**The Federal Administrative Court (BVGer — Switzerland's court of last resort for asylum appeals) held in a binding reference judgment, issued Tuesday 19 August, that young Afghan asylum seekers can be sent back to Afghanistan when "particularly favourable" individual circumstances apply.** In judgment D-3386/2026 the court updated its reading of conditions in Afghanistan since the Taliban's 2021 takeover: it found the security situation improved and civilian casualties sharply down, but the socio-economic and human-rights picture — especially for women and ethnic or religious minorities — still alarming, so removal remains "generally unreasonable" except in particularly favourable cases, a rule it now applies to the whole country. The case concerned a young, healthy Pashtun man with work experience and family support in Kabul, whose deportation the court upheld; it endorses the practice change the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) made in April 2025, and cannot be appealed to the Federal Supreme Court. The SEM had issued removal orders against some 200 Afghan men whose appeals were pending this leading decision, while 15,574 Afghans were registered in the asylum process at the end of July; the Swiss Refugee Council (SFH) criticised the ruling, calling returns to Taliban-ruled Afghanistan unreasonable in principle.   - Why it matters: The judgment sets the legal test that will govern hundreds of pending Afghan cases and future decisions, moving Switzerland from case-by-case caution toward routine returns — though a practical obstacle remains, since there are no direct flights to Afghanistan.
