- <a id="st-8d2d23abeafc" class="st-a"></a>**The Federal Council asked parliament on Wednesday 19 August for a supplementary credit of about 970 million francs to speed up the build-out of Switzerland's air defence, plus a further 100 million for civil protection.** The request (a Nachtragskredit — an in-year addition to the approved budget) breaks down as roughly 250 million francs for shorter-range ground-based air defence, 650 million for medium-range systems, 60 million for radar and 10 million for defending against mini-drones. The government says the money is needed for advance payments to lock in delivery slots, arguing that surging global demand has stretched arms-makers' production and lengthened lead times, so manufacturers now want substantial money up front. It cites a "heightened threat situation" as the justification. [SRF, 19 Aug 2026](https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/verschaerfte-bedrohungslage-bundesrat-will-970-mio-franken-mehr-fuer-die-luftverteidigung); [NZZ, 19 Aug 2026](https://www.nzz.ch/schweiz/armeebotschaft-2026-der-bundesrat-will-moegliche-angriffe-aus-der-luft-mit-einem-schweizer-drohnenabwehrsystem-bekaempfen-ld.1930274)
  - Why it matters: The credit is a concrete measure of how far Switzerland's post-neutrality-debate rearmament has moved from principle to cash — and it commits money before parliament has finished the larger fight over how to fund the army, including the temporary VAT rise the Council has separately proposed.

- <a id="st-5489a247c777" class="st-a"></a>**The switch to individual taxation in Switzerland will not take effect until 2032, the Federal Council confirmed on Wednesday 19 August** — years later than campaigners had hoped. The reform would tax spouses separately rather than jointly, ending the long-criticised "marriage penalty" under which some two-earner couples pay more than two unmarried earners on the same income; the delayed date reflects the scale of the changeover for cantonal tax authorities and IT systems. The reform still faces a likely referendum. [SRF, 19 Aug 2026](https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/einfuehrung-erst-spaeter-individualbesteuerung-tritt-erst-2032-in-kraft)
  - Why it matters: For two-income households in Switzerland this is the reform that decides whether marriage carries a tax cost — and a 2032 start means the current joint-taxation rules, and the penalty they can impose, stay in place for years yet.
