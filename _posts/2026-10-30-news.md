## 🇨🇭 Switzerland & Vaud

- <a id="st-39c1cd9a5632" class="st-a"></a>**President Guy Parmelin and Chinese President Xi Jinping on Friday 30 October oversaw the signing in Beijing of the upgraded Switzerland–China free trade agreement, the Federal Council said.** The update modernises the 2014 deal, which was the first free trade agreement China concluded with a European country. According to the government, it improves access to the Chinese market for Swiss goods and investment, and adds a legally binding chapter on trade and sustainable development covering environmental and labour rules, which the government says is a first in any Chinese free trade agreement. Two side agreements were signed as well: one on labour and employment cooperation and one that sets legal rules for exchanging research data between Swiss and Chinese researchers. The government says the two presidents also discussed human rights, noting that the latest session of the bilateral human-rights dialogue took place in Bern on 19–20 October. Parmelin's visit continues until Saturday 31 October, with meetings planned with China's commerce, labour and education ministers. [ongoing since 2026-10-26] [Federal Council, 30 Oct 2026](https://www.admin.ch/fr/newnsb/Nb4J2LzVCEaA) `[official PR]`
  Why it matters: China is Switzerland's third-largest trading partner after the EU and the US. The deal widens a trade channel for Swiss exporters after the US imposed steep tariffs on Swiss goods in 2025. The communiqué does not say how the new labour and environment chapter will be enforced.

- <a id="st-9bfb27820714" class="st-a"></a>**The Swiss National Bank reported a profit of 44.2 billion francs for the first nine months of 2026 in interim results published on Friday 30 October.** Almost all of it, 43.3 billion, came from its foreign-currency investments. Exchange-rate gains contributed 29.8 billion and equities 23.7 billion, while bonds lost 22.6 billion as interest rates rose worldwide. Gold, after a loss at mid-year, ended September with a valuation gain of 1.2 billion. The SNB repeats that its result depends largely on the gold, currency and capital markets, and that "only provisional conclusions are possible" about the full year. [SNB, 30 Oct 2026](https://www.snb.ch/en/publications/communication/press-releases/2026/pre_20261030) `[official PR]`
  Why it matters: the annual result decides whether the Confederation and the cantons receive a profit distribution next spring. That decision is still three months of market moves away, so the nine-month figure does not guarantee a payout.

- <a id="st-ee51014491dc" class="st-a"></a>**Electronic parts from eleven Swiss-based manufacturers keep turning up in new Russian weapons, including the jet-powered Geran-4 attack drone and the "Filin" cruise missile, according to a database of Ukraine's military intelligence agency HUR reported by SRF on Friday 30 October.** The parts are mass-produced civilian components such as microcontrollers, satellite-navigation processors, voltage converters and memory chips. They are not classed as war material, but the EU and G7 put them on a common sanctions list that Switzerland has adopted, so selling them to Russia is banned. Vladyslav Vlasiuk, an adviser to Ukraine's president, says Switzerland is doing too little and wants prosecutors brought in and export controls strengthened. The State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) told SRF that most of the parts are not made in Switzerland, so no exports from Switzerland take place, and that companies must contractually bar re-export to Russia. The firms SRF contacted, including STMicroelectronics (Plan-les-Ouates, GE) and u-blox (Thalwil, ZH), say they stopped all business with Russia in 2022. [SRF, 30 Oct 2026](https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/drohnen-krieg-in-der-ukraine-schweizer-bauteile-auch-in-neuen-russischen-waffen-entdeckt) [single-source]
  Why it matters: the parts reach Russia through distributors and middlemen in other countries, not through direct sales. That means the dispute is about how much responsibility a Swiss-headquartered firm has for its supply chain, not about breaking the export ban. Vlasiuk notes that parts from the Dutch chipmaker NXP have almost disappeared from Russian weapons, which suggests firms can do more.

Among the incidents were the disruption of a Pride church service in Zurich in June 2022 and of a children's drag reading at the Tanzhaus in October 2022. Prosecutors had asked for 15 months, six of them to be served. Three other members received suspended fines and one woman was acquitted on all counts. The leaders, whom SRF and Le Temps describe as heading a far-right group, said they had acted within freedom of expression. The verdicts can be appealed to the Zurich high court. ## 🌍 World politics & geopolitics

- <a id="st-fb7d688d5a1f" class="st-a"></a>**Final results of Israel's election of Tuesday 27 October, published overnight into Friday 30 October, confirm a hung Knesset: Benjamin Netanyahu's outgoing coalition has 53 of 120 seats and the opposition bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot has 48.** A government needs 61 seats. Netanyahu's Likud remains the largest party with 27 seats. The two Arab-led lists, Ra'am and the United List, won 19 seats between them, an unprecedented result, and turnout was close to 78%. Likud has alleged that Qatar paid to buy votes for the Arab parties. Qatar and the Arab parties reject the claim. Police said they had no evidence for it, and the Central Elections Committee called some of the circulating claims "entirely unfounded", adding that the separate Shin Bet investigation will not affect the results. Left-wing Democrats leader Yair Golan has met Eisenkot, Yair Lapid and Ra'am's Mansour Abbas and called for talks with the Arab-led parties. The results go to President Isaac Herzog on Wednesday 4 November, after which he will choose who gets the first chance to form a government.   Why it matters: neither bloc can govern without either a unity deal or support from the Arab-led parties, and the right-wing parties in Eisenkot's own bloc may resist relying on the latter. Which government emerges will largely decide whether Israel's policy in Gaza continues on its current course.
