- <a id="st-3b1c1bdf8ebb" class="st-a"></a>**The death toll from the high-speed train collision near Urda, in Spain's Toledo province, on Wednesday 28 October has risen to 69, with 58 injured, and the government says human error in handling a signalling failure caused it.** At a press conference on Wednesday, transport minister Óscar Puente set out the sequence. At about 08:30 a computer fault at Urda knocked out LZB, the line's 1990s-era in-cab safety system that keeps trains a safe distance apart, and six trains on a 69 km stretch were left to controllers at the Atocha traffic centre run by the rail operator Adif. Audio recordings obtained by El País show that at 08:36 a controller told the driver of Renfe's AVE 2080 (397 passengers) to carry on "with normal running", relying on lineside signals. Iryo train 6076 (147 passengers) was standing at the Urda entry signal, and the AVE hit it at 08:53. At 200 km/h a train of that size needs about 4 km to stop, which is too far for a driver who can only rely on what they see. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez declared three days of official mourning, running until midnight on Saturday 31 October. It is the second fatal crash on the Madrid–Seville line this year, after 46 people died at Adamuz on 18 January. LZB is due to be replaced by ERTMS, the European standard train-control system.
