## 🇨🇭 Switzerland & Vaud

- <a id="st-cc4cae02f4ab" class="st-a"></a>**Swiss voters headed to a clear double rejection of the neutrality and food-security initiatives on Sunday 27 September, according to the first projections.** DemoSquare's projection for Le Temps at 12:05 put the "Safeguard Swiss neutrality" initiative at 70.2% No and the "For safe food" initiative at 72.6% No, with no canton accepting either; the SRG trend computed by gfs.bern likewise called both a "clear No." The neutrality text — launched by the sovereigntist group Pro Suisse, co-authored by former federal councillor Christoph Blocher and backed among major parties only by the SVP (Swiss People's Party) — would have written a strictly defined "perpetual armed neutrality" into the constitution; its backers argued Bern had drifted too close to the EU and NATO since Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine. The food initiative asked the Confederation to reach at least 70% net food self-sufficiency within ten years by promoting a more plant-based diet. A gfs.bern poll on 16 September had already shown the neutrality initiative trailing 34% to 63%. Why it matters: rejection leaves Switzerland's flexible, unwritten practice of neutrality intact and rebuffs an attempt to lock foreign-policy latitude into the constitution — the outcome that most directly shapes how Bern can position itself between the EU, NATO and Russia. ## 🌍 World politics & geopolitics

- <a id="st-fc4d464c704d" class="st-a"></a>**Iran said on Sunday 27 September it would not drop its conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, a day after US President Donald Trump publicly rejected Tehran's seven-day plan.** Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told state broadcaster IRIB that "any move toward reopening the Strait of Hormuz is contingent upon these conditions being fulfilled," while adding Iran had not closed the door to negotiations. The plan, floated at the UN General Assembly and passed to Washington via Qatari mediators, ties a reopening to a ceasefire across the Middle East, the release of frozen Iranian assets, the lifting of oil sanctions and an end to the US naval blockade. Trump, speaking at the White House on Saturday, said "I reject their proposal" and claimed Iran was "losing so badly" — though, as DW notes, the waterway remains largely shut to international shipping despite his separate claim that the US controls it. Roughly a fifth of global oil demand passed through Hormuz before the war. Why it matters: with the strait still effectively closed and the two sides talking only through mediators, the standoff keeps a chokepoint for global oil and gas supply unresolved.
