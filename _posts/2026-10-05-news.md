## 🇨🇭 Switzerland & Vaud

- <a id="st-75769dc44236" class="st-a"></a>**The Federal Council on Monday 5 October launched its campaign for the federal vote on Sunday 29 November on raising VAT to help finance the 13th AHV/AVS pension payment (the extra annual old-age pension voters approved in 2024).** Under the bill passed by Parliament, the standard VAT rate would rise 0.4 points, from 8.1% to 8.5%, and the accommodation rate from 3.8% to 4.0%. The reduced 2.6% rate for food, medicines, newspapers and other everyday goods would stay the same. The government estimates the 13th payment, first paid in December 2026, will cost more than CHF 4 billion a year at first and over CHF 5 billion from 2035. The VAT rise would bring in about CHF 1.5 billion a year, so it covers only part of that: the projected 2035 deficit would fall from about CHF 5 billion to about CHF 3 billion, and the AHV fund would stand at about 70% of annual spending instead of 50%. Opponents, led by the FDP, say the rise would hurt household purchasing power and small businesses. They want the cost dealt with through spending cuts in the next AHV reform. **Why it matters:** the vote decides whether everyone pays for the new pension payment through consumption, which affects every household's costs, or whether its deficit is left to the next pension reform. The campaigners, including Public Eye, object mainly to lower tariffs on Malaysian palm oil, which they link to rainforest clearing, displacement and forced labour. The Economic Affairs Department (EAER) says the deal gives Swiss firms more legal certainty and better access to the Malaysian market. **Why it matters:** this is the second Swiss popular vote on palm oil in a trade deal, after the narrow approval of the Indonesia agreement in 2021, and it will test whether certification-linked tariff cuts are acceptable to voters.
