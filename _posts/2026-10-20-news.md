- <a id="st-8d6777d4b46a" class="st-a"></a>**Albertans voted by roughly three to one on Monday 19 October against starting a process that could lead to the Canadian province leaving Canada.** Le Monde reports 76.5% for staying in Canada and 23.4% for opening a path towards a second, binding independence referendum. Elections Alberta said 1,356,873 people (43.1% of 3,148,343 eligible electors) had already voted in advance or by mail before polling day, against 809,988 at the 2023 provincial election. Why it matters: the result shuts down, for now, a secession push that US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had encouraged by calling Alberta Washington's "natural partner", in the middle of a US–Canada trade war. - <a id="st-a172b46305cc" class="st-a"></a>**Swiss exports rose 2.6% in the third quarter to CHF 75.0 billion (seasonally adjusted), and imports reached a record CHF 61.0 billion, the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (FOCBS) said on Tuesday 20 October.** In real terms (adjusted for prices), exports grew 3.3% and imports fell 1.4%. Why it matters: Q2 pharma exports drove the growth that led SECO to raise its 2026 GDP forecast to 1.7%, and that boost is now fading on the US side. - <a id="st-ddebfb0aa3b1" class="st-a"></a>**The referendum against lifting Switzerland's ban on new nuclear power plants has formally qualified, the Federal Chancellery said on Monday 19 October, so voters will have the final say.** Of 79,479 signatures filed on 6 October, 78,922 were valid; 50,000 are needed. The target is the 19 June 2026 amendment to the Nuclear Energy Act, Parliament's indirect counter-proposal (an alternative law offered instead of an initiative) to the "Stop Blackout" initiative. It would remove the ban on new plants that voters backed in 2017 as part of Energy Strategy 2050. No vote date has been set. Why it matters: the national vote will decide whether new nuclear can again be part of Switzerland's long-term electricity plans. - <a id="st-01cb701273ac" class="st-a"></a>**From Monday 2 November 2026, Swiss citizens can order a new biometric identity card with a chip storing a facial image and two fingerprints, the Federal Council announced on Monday 19 October.** The fee stays the same as for today's card. The chipless card will still be issued, and existing cards remain valid for EU travel until they expire (at most ten years for adults), as set out in the Bilaterals III package. The government recommends the biometric card for people who travel in the EU, since the EU has issued only biometric ID cards since 2021. It is a separate thing from the e-ID, which is a digital identity and not a travel document. On the same date, the "provisional passport" becomes the "emergency passport". [Federal Council / admin.ch, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.admin.ch/fr/newnsb/j651IXJTSFty)

Lot 2 is public cloud with data processed and stored only in Switzerland and built on open-source software (two awards). Lot 3 is an on-premise private cloud in federal data centres (one award). Contracts run for seven years. Why it matters: the one-lot-per-bidder rule is how Bern is putting its digital-sovereignty directive into practice, by making sure no single provider or legal jurisdiction controls the government's cloud. [FOITT / admin.ch, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.admin.ch/fr/newnsb/vL4DxR3WIhey)
