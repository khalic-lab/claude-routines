- <a id="st-271c00f49144" class="st-a"></a>**Switzerland's defence department (DDPS) has extended its counter-espionage rules to keep connected cars away from sensitive military sites, RTS reported on 29 July.** The restrictions apply near installations such as the army's electronic-warfare centre at Zimmerwald (Bern), though the DDPS would not detail them. They follow the first-ever warning by the Federal Intelligence Service (SRC), in its annual security report, about connected vehicles — especially Chinese ones; SRC director Serge Bavaud said on 25 June that people likely to interest foreign spies should avoid Chinese connected cars, calling them "smartphones on wheels" whose cameras and microphones could hand recorded conversations to Chinese intelligence. Carmaker BYD says it strictly complies with EU and Swiss data-protection rules and transmits no data to China. Why it matters: Switzerland joins Germany — where Bavaria has opened an inquiry — and the US, which banned imports of Chinese and Russian connected vehicles in 2025, in treating cheap, data-rich cars as a surveillance risk.
