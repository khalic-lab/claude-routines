It would require both chambers to ratify the new premiums each autumn session when the average rise exceeds 3%. If a referendum is called, premiums would be frozen until the vote. Centrist national councillor Benjamin Roduit argues that voters who reject a rise would also have to say which benefits to cut, which risks a deadlock. RTS says the Council of States could debate the motion in December. Why it matters: premiums are a large and rising cost for every household in Switzerland. The motion would give voters a direct check on them, but the government says it would treat the symptom rather than the cost growth behind it.
