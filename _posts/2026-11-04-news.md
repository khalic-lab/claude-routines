- <a id="st-1bfd12f8050b" class="st-a"></a>**Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate in the US midterm elections on Tuesday 3 November, the first time since 2006 that a sitting Republican president has lost both chambers.** By the Associated Press tally relayed by Al Jazeera on Wednesday morning, Democrats had won 228 House seats and led in 34 more (218 is a majority), and had won 54 Senate seats (51 is a majority); counting continues and several races are uncalled. They flipped Senate seats in states Republicans have long held: James Talarico beat Ken Paxton in Texas, Adam Hamilton became the first Democrat elected to the Senate from Kansas since 1932, Troy Jackson unseated Susan Collins in Maine, and Abdul El-Sayed beat Mike Rogers in Michigan to become the first Muslim elected to the Senate. Speaking to Fox News, Donald Trump congratulated the Democrats and said he would accept the results, but added "I wasn't on the ballot", and he posted an image of a red cap reading "Veto" on Truth Social. Why it matters: from January, legislation, budgets and investigations will need a Democratic Congress, so Trump will have to rely on his veto and on executive action for the second half of his term, including over the Iran war. - <a id="st-c769d63b86ae" class="st-a"></a>**Prosecutors cannot order internet providers to block websites except where a specific law allows it, the Federal Supreme Court said on Wednesday 4 November, upholding an appeal by the provider Init7 against the Vaud public prosecutor.** In July 2025 the Vaud prosecutor ordered several providers to block access to the web addresses of an alleged online investment fraud (a DNS block, which stops the domain name from resolving). SRF reports that the fraud cost investors more than CHF 1.7 million. The prosecutor relied on the general rules on seizure in the Criminal Procedure Code. The court, in a judgment dated Monday 12 October (7B_52/2026), ruled that these rules are not a sufficient legal basis. Parliament has allowed blocking only in specific cases: unlicensed online gambling, illegal pornography and, temporarily, domains in unfair-competition proceedings. The court said blocking can be an effective tool against online fraud, but that deciding when to allow it, who orders it and on what conditions is for the federal legislator. Why it matters: Swiss prosecutors lose a tool they had been using against online scams, and whether to give it back is now a question for parliament. It points to pension funds' average returns of 6.1% in 2025 and 5.4% to the end of August 2026, and an average funding ratio of 122.5% for funds without a state guarantee. In its words, the increase "compensates the relatively high inflation observed since 2022". The social partners had argued for rates from 1.25% to 3%. The minimum applies only to the mandatory part of retirement savings; funds set their own rate on savings above that level. Why it matters: most employees in Switzerland will see a higher guaranteed return on their pension savings next year, and the rate is now above October's 1.5% inflation.
