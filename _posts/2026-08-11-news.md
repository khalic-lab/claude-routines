- <a id="st-d4ef2eebc6cc" class="st-a"></a>**A magnitude-7.4 earthquake devastated western Colombia on Monday 10 August, killing scores of people and triggering a frantic overnight search for survivors under collapsed buildings.** The US Geological Survey put the quake at M7.4, striking 5 km south of San José del Palmar in the remote Chocó region at 12:34 UTC, followed by a M5.0 aftershock and, per Colombia's Geological Service, at least 21 aftershocks by late Monday. President Abelardo de la Espriella — sworn in only on Friday 7 August — reported 35 dead in Cali and declared a state of emergency; local authorities counted at least 40 more dead around Pereira and a dozen elsewhere, with 700 injured in the Valle del Cauca department alone. More than 2,700 people were "reported missing" on citizen-run websites by Monday night, a figure authorities caution is unverified and not a confirmed count. Chocó, near the epicentre and reachable mainly by boat or plane, remains largely unassessed. **Why it matters:** it is Colombia's strongest quake in decades, striking a mountainous, hard-to-reach zone where the true toll may take days to emerge, and the first major test of a government four days into office.
