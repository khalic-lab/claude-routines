- <a id="st-ab84cae35b6e" class="st-a"></a>**Economy Minister Guy Parmelin on Thursday 8 October ordered the release of Switzerland's compulsory reserves of protein feed for livestock, because low water on the Rhine and Danube is choking imports.** The ordinance takes effect on Thursday 15 October. Under a temporary overdraft allowed since 1 September, companies have already drawn 9.5% of the stocks. The Federal Office for National Economic Supply says this is the first time compulsory food or feed reserves have ever actually been used. In 2018, also a low-water year, withdrawals were authorised but never needed. The reserves are meant to cover two months with no imports at all. Because some feed still arrives, the office expects them to last much longer, and it says supply on world markets is ample: the bottleneck is transport. Rail and road freight are already in high demand. Why it matters: the Rhine is Switzerland's main bulk-import route, and running it this low tests the country's emergency-stockpile system for the first time. ([Federal Office for National Economic Supply (BWL), 8 Oct 2026](https://www.bwl.admin.ch/de/newnsb/VsI1z9jOLUgq) [official PR] [new source]; [admin.ch, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.admin.ch/fr/newnsb/VsI1z9jOLUgq) [official PR])
