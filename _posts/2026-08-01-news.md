- <a id="st-2eba3f3baf16" class="st-a"></a>**Defence Minister Martin Pfister has apologised in writing to Switzerland's churches for stripping clergy of their exemption from military service without consulting them first, according to a copy of the letter obtained by RTS and reported Friday 31 July.** Since 1 June, priests and pastors have no longer been exempt under a change to the Military Act that the Federal Council introduced without prior announcement or parliamentary debate, so quietly that it went unnoticed at first; nine clergy have since been called up to serve their remaining days. "The Defence group made a mistake by not consulting you on the revision of the Military Act. It clearly should have. The Council had justified dropping the exemption by arguing that pastoral care no longer holds the same significance for civil society; Pfister's letter now softens that, acknowledging clergy's value "in crisis situations", and Lugano diocese administrator Alain de Raemy welcomed the apology. - <a id="st-5942ea8b0f5f" class="st-a"></a>**The Swiss defence department (VBS) has barred permanently internet-connected vehicles — Chinese models in particular — from the vicinity of especially sensitive military sites, RTS reported on Friday 31 July.** One such site is the electronic-warfare centre at Zimmerwald near Bern; the VBS declined to detail the access restrictions, saying they depend on each location's security setup. The move follows an unusually blunt warning from the Federal Intelligence Service (NDB), whose director Serge Bavaud told a late-June press conference that connected cars are "smartphones on wheels" whose manufacturers cooperate with Chinese authorities, giving intelligence services potential access to recorded data including conversations. Carmaker BYD says it transmits no data to China and "strictly respects" all European and Swiss data-protection rules; other Chinese makers did not respond to RTS. Switzerland is not alone — German intelligence has run tests on several Chinese brands and the state of Bavaria has opened an inquiry. ([SRF, 31 Jul 2026](https://www.srf.ch/news/dialog/vernetzte-autos-aus-china-armee-ergreift-schutzmassnahmen-gegen-smartphones-auf-raedern))
  Why it matters: It is a concrete instance of geopolitical mistrust reaching into everyday consumer technology on Swiss soil, and a signal to buyers of increasingly popular Chinese EVs that the state now treats their onboard sensors as a surveillance surface.
