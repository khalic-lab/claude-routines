- <a id="st-rent-initiative" class="st-a"></a>**The Federal Council decided on Friday 25 September to reject the tenants' "rent-price initiative" but to draft an indirect counter-proposal setting clearer legal rules against abusive rents.** The initiative — submitted on 23 June 2026 by the Swiss tenants' association — would require rents to be checked automatically and regularly and abusive ones to be lowered; a rent counts as abusive when it exceeds actual costs plus a reasonable return. The government called that automatic-control mechanism "very laborious" and warned it could cut investment in housing construction without effectively curbing abuse. Instead it acknowledged that today's rules on abusive rents are "complex and sometimes opaque," and said its counter-proposal — not yet drafted — would write clearer, more transparent criteria into law so tenants and landlords can more quickly tell whether a rent is excessive. Why it matters: the choice sets up the familiar Swiss fault line between enforceable rent caps, which tenants want, and lighter transparency rules favoured by landlords — and because it is an indirect counter-proposal rather than the initiative itself, any concrete relief for renters would arrive only after the legislative process runs its course. - <a id="st-votes-27-sep" class="st-a"></a>**Switzerland votes on Sunday 27 September on two federal initiatives — the neutrality initiative and the food-security initiative — alongside cantonal and communal ballots.** The neutrality initiative ("Preservation of Swiss neutrality") would write a stricter definition into the constitution: Switzerland would stay permanently neutral, join no military alliance, and not take part in sanctions against warring states — a direct constraint on the kind of EU-aligned sanctions Bern has adopted over Russia. The food-security initiative would commit the country to raising its self-sufficiency rate to at least 70% through more sustainable domestic production, a shift toward plant-based food, and clean drinking water. Polling by gfs.bern in the run-up showed the neutrality initiative losing support. Why it matters: the neutrality vote in particular would bind future foreign-policy choices at a moment when Switzerland's alignment with EU sanctions and its ties to the bloc are already contested at home.
