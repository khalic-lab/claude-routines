- <a id="st-563b7c097959" class="st-a"></a>**The United Nations said on Friday 25 September it was "gravely concerned" as fighting spread across multiple fronts in northern Ethiopia, warning that the country risks sliding back into full-scale war less than three years after a 2022 peace deal.** UN spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric said the organisation was "particularly troubled by reports of the use of drones as well as incidents of harm to civilians," and rights chief Volker Türk said he was "deeply alarmed," citing airstrikes that hit five schools in Tigray on 22 September and a mortar strike that killed civilians in Kobo, in the neighbouring Amhara region, two days later. Secretary-General António Guterres and his Horn of Africa envoy, Guang Cong, are pressing both sides, working with the AU and the regional bloc IGAD, to restart dialogue under that accord. Why it matters: a collapse of the Pretoria deal would reopen one of the deadliest conflicts of the past decade and destabilise the wider Horn of Africa, and the drone strikes and communications blackout make it far harder for outsiders to verify what is happening or get aid in.
