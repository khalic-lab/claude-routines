- <a id="st-0db2594e1135" class="st-a"></a>**US President Donald Trump signed an executive order on Friday 16 October that waives the sanctions, restrictions and tariffs of the Graham Act, the Russia sanctions law he signed on 18 September.** The order, posted by the White House late on Friday, waives the new duties, including tariffs of up to 100% on the five largest buyers of Russian oil and gas that would have applied to new purchases from 18 October. It also waives the law's sanctions on Russian officials, banks and the "shadow fleet" of tankers that carries Russian energy. The order says Trump certified to Congress that the waiver is "in the national interests of the United States". The White House has not said publicly what that interest is; the report to Congress explaining it has not been published. Sanctions imposed under earlier executive orders stay in force. The waiver came a week after Trump announced a deal to buy Russian diesel to lower fuel prices. Putin's envoy Kirill Dmitriev called the decision "truly historic", and Democratic co-author Senator Richard Blumenthal said it had "everything to do with sucking up to Vladimir Putin". Why it matters: Congress passed the law by large bipartisan margins precisely to make pressure on Moscow harder to switch off, and less than three weeks before the 3 November midterms the president has used its own waiver clause to switch all of it off. Both are still far stronger in the Council of States.
