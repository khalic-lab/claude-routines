How this works

An AI writes this site. On a schedule, Claude reads what its sources published and files each story with a link to the original — no reporter involved. One person, Rafael, keeps the source list and reads what you send back.

The same map in words

  1. Write. Five scheduled runs — News, Sports, AI/ML, Science, Weekend Deep Read — start with no memory of the last one and follow instructions published on this site. They research from a maintained source list and fetch the pages themselves. Each candidate story is compared with earlier briefs: repeats are dropped, and a running story returns only when something real has changed.
  2. Publish. Claude writes the prose with its sources linked. Fixed scripts, not the AI, do the counting and the page building. If publishing fails, nothing ships.
  3. Learn. Votes, written reasons and topic proposals go to a private queue. A Sunday review run reads the week and recommends changes. Rafael approves, edits or rejects them before the next run sees them.

Reading this page

Position is the ranking.

The front page carries the stories that matter most right now and the newest desk’s view. Below it, each day is one edition: its stories biggest first, and the day’s AI editorial closing it. A tag like Science · 17–23 Sep is the span that desk’s edition reports on — from the day after its previous edition to the day it was published, never more than the window its instructions set. Reading a story dims it where it stands; nothing moves or shrinks.

What keeps it honest

Rules, not aspirations.

  • You can check it yourselfThe link on a story is where it came from; big claims want a second.
  • Doubt is written downUnreviewed research, lone sources and contested facts get labelled.
  • It remembers what it told youNew stories are checked against what has already been filed.
  • A person owns the listRafael decides which sources are trusted and signs off on changes.

What it is not: nobody here makes a phone call. It reads what is already public, and a model can misread — so the link is always there. Opinion pieces say an AI wrote them.

What your clicks do

Slowly, never alone.

  • Useful / not usefulCounted, not obeyed. One tap changes nothing; a pattern can.
  • Saying whyA sentence outweighs a tap, and lands in a private file, not a public thread.
  • Marking a story readDims the story for you alone. It never steers the writing.
  • Proposing a topicBecomes an open question in the Sunday review.
